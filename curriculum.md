# Curriculum

## 1. Real-time Speaking

Practice:
- spontaneous answers;
- short workplace conversations;
- clarification questions;
- explaining an analytical workflow;
- giving opinions;
- describing problems and solutions;
- interview follow-up questions;
- reacting without preparation;
- politely agreeing, disagreeing, challenging, and qualifying a statement;
- natural short responses in meetings and scheduling conversations.

Progression:
- 1 sentence;
- 2-3 sentences;
- 3-5 stable sentences;
- 4-6 sentences;
- follow-up questions;
- realistic interview-style dialogue.

The priority is stability and automaticity, not simply increasing answer length.

## 2. Prepared Speaking

Practice structured answers on:
- Tell me about yourself;
- professional background;
- strengths and weaknesses;
- why this company / role;
- why analytics;
- successful experiments;
- failed experiments;
- difficult analytical tasks;
- stakeholder communication;
- data-quality problems;
- product decisions;
- A/B testing methodology;
- causal inference and experimentation trade-offs;
- giving a short status update;
- explaining findings or recommendations to colleagues;
- politely clarifying, challenging, or disagreeing with a stakeholder;
- explaining uncertainty, trade-offs, and limitations;
- describing personal contribution and impact using concise situation → action → result structure.

Target: concise, natural answers that can be reproduced under interview or workplace conditions.

## 3. Grammar Drills

Use *English Grammar in Use* as the broad reference map, but practice topics in mixed order.

### High-value recurring areas

- articles;
- conditionals (2nd, 3rd, mixed);
- modal verbs;
- modal perfects: must have / can't have / might have;
- gerund vs infinitive;
- verb complementation patterns;
- relative clauses;
- passive voice and perfect passive forms;
- reported speech;
- countable / uncountable nouns;
- less vs fewer;
- used to / be used to / get used to;
- prefer / would rather;
- would rather + bare infinitive;
- would rather + person + past simple;
- would rather + person + past perfect;
- linking words of contrast and concession;
- although / though / despite / in spite of / whereas / while;
- sentence-final `though`;
- however / nevertheless;
- common fixed-preposition patterns;
- article use in professional speech.

### Verb-pattern track

Regularly practice common high-value patterns in professional contexts, including:
- consider doing;
- suggest doing;
- deny doing;
- regret doing;
- can't help doing;
- remember doing vs remember to do;
- decide to do / decide not to do;
- manage to do;
- afford to do;
- allow someone to do;
- prevent someone from doing;
- warn someone against doing;
- apologize for doing;
- think about/of doing.

Do not treat this as a finite list; expand it when recurring errors appear.

### Fixed prepositions and collocations

Regularly recycle patterns such as:
- consistent with;
- indicative of;
- result in / result from;
- account for;
- provide someone with;
- take into account;
- successful in doing;
- lead to;
- explained by;
- prevent someone from doing.

### Coverage reference

The systematic grammar coverage map is *English Grammar in Use*, fifth edition (145 units). The teaching stages, exercise-selection rules, status transitions and unit-number validation belong to `SKILL.md`; the **current status of each unit** belongs only to `state.md`. The topic lists above are a long-term syllabus, not a claim that each topic is presently weak.

## 5. Retelling

Two complementary content streams:
- **Professional / product retelling:** product analytics stories, incident investigations, A/B tests, product decisions, operational problems, workplace situations, interview-style examples.
- **IELTS-style topic retelling:** broad everyday experiences, people, places and non-work themes for transfer beyond professional vocabulary.

### Long-term retelling skills

- identify the main idea and preserve the logical sequence;
- retain causes, consequences, trade-offs and key details;
- compress non-essential information;
- paraphrase instead of reproducing exact wording;
- use natural transitions and tense consistency;
- keep sentences manageable and reuse learned structures when natural.

### IELTS Topic Retelling — canonical category plan

1. People
2. Places
3. Experiences
4. Work & study
5. Technology
6. Objects
7. Media & culture
8. Activities
9. Communication
10. Future plans

A category can have multiple distinct stories. Completing one story does not mean the whole category is mastered. The **story protocol, correction cycle and difficulty adaptation** belong to `SKILL.md`; category/story attempts and their statuses belong to `state.md`.

## 6. Lexical Reconstruction

Active retrieval of lexical chunks from short texts through progressively reduced contextual support.

Learning targets:
- high-frequency collocations and multiword expressions;
- phrasal verbs and useful verb patterns;
- prepositions and natural linking phrases;
- reconstruction of grammatical, idiomatic sentences from minimal clues;
- recognition of natural paraphrases and transfer to spontaneous speaking.

This mode can use the same professional, everyday, and IELTS-style subject areas as Retelling, but is not required before every retelling. The **four-stage exercise protocol** is defined only in `SKILL.md`; actual recurring lexical gaps or transfer evidence belong in `state.md`.

## 7. Transfer Targets

The curriculum aims for both **controlled accuracy** and **unprompted production** in realistic work, interview and general-purpose speech. Use the transfer protocol in `SKILL.md` and record the actual observed ability in `state.md`.
