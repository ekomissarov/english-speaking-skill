# Curriculum

## 1. Real-time Speaking

Practice:
- spontaneous answers;
- short workplace conversations;
- clarification questions;
- explaining an analytical workflow;
- giving opinions;
- describing problems and solutions;
- interview follow-up questions;
- reacting without preparation;
- politely agreeing, disagreeing, challenging, and qualifying a statement;
- natural short responses in meetings and scheduling conversations.

Progression:
- 1 sentence;
- 2-3 sentences;
- 3-5 stable sentences;
- 4-6 sentences;
- follow-up questions;
- realistic interview-style dialogue.

The priority is stability and automaticity, not simply increasing answer length.

## 2. Prepared Speaking

Practice structured answers on:
- Tell me about yourself;
- professional background;
- strengths and weaknesses;
- why this company / role;
- why analytics;
- successful experiments;
- failed experiments;
- difficult analytical tasks;
- stakeholder communication;
- data-quality problems;
- product decisions;
- A/B testing methodology;
- causal inference and experimentation trade-offs;
- giving a short status update;
- explaining findings or recommendations to colleagues;
- politely clarifying, challenging, or disagreeing with a stakeholder;
- explaining uncertainty, trade-offs, and limitations;
- describing personal contribution and impact using concise situation → action → result structure.

Target: concise, natural answers that can be reproduced under interview or workplace conditions.

## 3. Grammar Drills

Use *English Grammar in Use* as the broad reference map, but practice topics in mixed order.

### High-value recurring areas

- articles;
- conditionals (2nd, 3rd, mixed);
- modal verbs;
- modal perfects: must have / can't have / might have;
- gerund vs infinitive;
- verb complementation patterns;
- relative clauses;
- passive voice and perfect passive forms;
- reported speech;
- countable / uncountable nouns;
- less vs fewer;
- used to / be used to / get used to;
- prefer / would rather;
- would rather + bare infinitive;
- would rather + person + past simple;
- would rather + person + past perfect;
- linking words of contrast and concession;
- although / though / despite / in spite of / whereas / while;
- sentence-final `though`;
- however / nevertheless;
- common fixed-preposition patterns;
- article use in professional speech.

### Verb-pattern track

Regularly practice common high-value patterns in professional contexts, including:
- consider doing;
- suggest doing;
- deny doing;
- regret doing;
- can't help doing;
- remember doing vs remember to do;
- decide to do / decide not to do;
- manage to do;
- afford to do;
- allow someone to do;
- prevent someone from doing;
- warn someone against doing;
- apologize for doing;
- think about/of doing.

Do not treat this as a finite list; expand it when recurring errors appear.

### Fixed prepositions and collocations

Regularly recycle patterns such as:
- consistent with;
- indicative of;
- result in / result from;
- account for;
- provide someone with;
- take into account;
- successful in doing;
- lead to;
- explained by;
- prevent someone from doing.

### Grammar selection policy

Choose topics using this priority:
1. recurring learner errors;
2. weak topics;
3. recently learned structures that need consolidation;
4. high-frequency spoken grammar;
5. business-English usefulness;
6. uncovered textbook areas;
7. spaced repetition of previously learned structures.

When a topic becomes reliable in controlled exercises, reduce direct drilling and test it later through spontaneous speaking and retelling.

## 4. Professional Vocabulary and Phrasal Verbs

Build active command of high-frequency professional phrasal verbs and multi-word expressions.

Current core set:
- bring up;
- point out;
- put off;
- find out;
- rule out;
- come across;
- sort out;
- run into;
- follow up;
- carry out;
- look into;
- look over;
- go over;
- hold off;
- call off;
- phase out;
- cut down on;
- set up;
- back up;
- step in;
- break down;
- end up;
- work out;
- take up;
- deal with;
- go through.

Progression:
1. recognize meaning;
2. complete or translate a short sentence;
3. produce a sentence from a situation;
4. use the expression naturally in an open-ended professional answer;
5. recycle it later in a different context.

Avoid endless translation drills once recognition is stable.

## 5. Retelling

Method:

**Chunk → Keywords → Retell**

Progression:
- 20-30 second fragment;
- consolidate accuracy at the current length;
- 30-45 seconds;
- 45-60 seconds;
- longer passages.

Focus on:
- identifying the main idea;
- keeping the logical sequence;
- preserving causal relationships and trade-offs;
- compressing details;
- retelling in different words;
- using natural linking phrases;
- maintaining tense consistency;
- keeping sentences manageable rather than over-complex;
- reusing recently practiced grammar and vocabulary when natural.

Suggested content:
- product analytics stories;
- short business cases;
- experiment results;
- workplace situations;
- general-interest stories;
- interview-style examples.

Do not increase length simply because meaning retention is good. Increase difficulty when both retention and language stability are reasonably comfortable.

## 6. Transfer and Consolidation

The curriculum should deliberately transfer material between modes:

- errors from Real-time Speaking → targeted Grammar/Prepared Speaking;
- grammar mastered in drills → open-ended speaking;
- phrasal verbs and collocations → interview/workplace answers;
- useful Prepared Speaking patterns → later Real-time Speaking;
- recently practiced grammar/vocabulary → Retelling when natural.

The goal is automatic use, not isolated exercise performance.
